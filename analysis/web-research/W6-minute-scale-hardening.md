# W6: Minute-scale hardening. Strengthening hidden tests only, re-grading the episodes already captured, and confirming honestly (web research, 2026-10-04)

The question. A screen already captured every episode's final repository for every step, along with the
agent trajectory. Suppose the instruction and fixture (everything the agent sees) stay byte-identical. Then
the agent's behaviour distribution does not depend on the hidden tests, so we could strengthen only the
hidden suite and re-grade the captured episodes offline in minutes, with no redesign and no re-screen.

This file covers:

- what the test-amplification literature did and how much it moved scores (§1);
- whether mining cases from captured solver outputs can be made legitimate, given `harden.py`'s rule that
  only aggregate per-check rates reach a redesign (§2);
- a concrete "hidden-only fast hardening" design for this pipeline (§3);
- validity risks and their safeguards (§4);
- a time budget per step (§5).

Companion files: W2 §1.15 and §4 and W4 §5 already summarise PatchDiff, UTBoost, STING, SWE-ABS, EvalPlus and
PBT as *techniques*. This file adds what those summaries left out: how each paper *re-graded existing outputs*,
whether its tests were mined from those same outputs, its false-positive controls and costs, and the
statistics of adaptive reuse.

Test *generation* from the issue alone is weak, which is why every working method above runs code against an
oracle. Otter++ produces fail-to-pass tests for 37.0% of SWT-bench Verified instances, and the later e-Otter++
reaches 62.1% [S] ([2502.05368](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05368), [SWT-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12952)).
On TestGenEval, GPT-4o's whole-file suites reach 35.2% coverage and an 18.8% mutation score [S]
([2410.00752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00752)).

**What 1.1 means for us [I].**

- **Where it bites.** Tests mined from gold variants (STING, SWE-ABS) and tests mined from the evaluated
  outputs (UTBoost, PatchDiff) both bite. Only the former yields an out-of-sample re-grade.
- **Divergence is not failure.** PatchDiff's 66.2% "uncertain" is the number to remember. When a solver's
  output differs from the reference on a generated input, that is *usually not* a settled failure. It is
  usually an under-specified point.
- **Even gold-only mining over-fits the reference.** SWE-ABS still found 10.6% of instances with gold-specific
  tests, and the OpenAI audit found 35.5% narrow tests on hard tasks.
- **Near misses are the best mutants.** Mutants that come from real agent near misses are much harder to kill
  than operator mutants (SWE-Mutation 71% → 40%). The captured episodes are therefore the most informative
  mutant source we have, and also the most dangerous one statistically (§2).

**What 1.2 means for us [I].**

- **Mine generators, not cases.** Have the miner write *generators* over the published domain (LiveCodeBench,
  CodeContests+), never literal cases.
- **Validate inputs from the instruction alone.** Each generated input must pass a *validator written from the
  instruction alone* (auditors, CodeContests+). This is the executable form of "within published rules".
- **Expected values come from consensus.** They come from agreement among independent implementations
  (rStar-Coder, TestCase-Eval, auditors), never from one implementation and never from an LLM's prediction
  (CodeHacker).
- **Where several outputs are valid, use a checker.** Where the published rule admits more than one output,
  the test must be a *checker*, not an equality (CodeContests+: about a quarter of problems).
- **Expect a fifth of first-pass findings to be wrong.** About 21% of agent-found failures did not survive
  certification even with a strong consensus oracle. Budget for that.

### 1.3 The oracle problem, N-version consensus and its limit

- **Survey.** Barr et al. (IEEE TSE 41(5), 2015) [S]
  ([pdf](https://coinse.github.io/publications/pdfs/Barr2015qd.pdf)) classify automated oracles into
  specified, derived, implicit and human. Pseudo-oracles (independent versions), metamorphic relations and
  contracts are all *derived* oracles, and the human remains the final oracle when automation is inadequate.
- **Knight and Leveson (1986)** [S]. 27 independent versions of one specification were run on 1M random inputs.
  Coincident failures occurred far more often than independence predicts (z ≈ 100).
- **N-version programming with coding agents** (2026) [V~]
  ([2606.20158](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20158v1)) is the direct replication with Claude Code, Codex,
  Cursor, Gemini and OpenCode over 23 models.
  - **429 coincident failures where independence predicts 115.36** (z = 29.2).
  - **Crossing an agent or vendor boundary does not decorrelate**: 87 of 158 defined cross-agent pairs had
    perfectly correlated failure profiles (ϕ = 1).
  - The dominant common-mode failures were **shared misreadings of the specification**, such as computing a
    circumcircle instead of a minimum enclosing circle.
  - Majority-vote triples still cut mean failures from 387 to 131, and 68.5% of triples had zero failures.
  - **Implication [I].** Agreement between the reference and the alternative is a necessary filter but not
    proof. The failure it cannot catch is exactly the one that matters here: both implementations and the
    reviewer read a sentence the same wrong way. The determinacy probe in §3 targets that hole.
- **Intramorphic testing** (Rigger and Su 2022, as used by UTBoost) [V] compares the original and the modified
  program on the same input. That is our reference-vs-episode differential.
- **Metamorphic testing** [S] ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphic_testing); surveys by Segura
  et al., TSE 2016, and Chen et al., ACM CSUR 2018) removes the need for an expected value. The relation is the
  oracle, for example f(alias form) = f(canonical form). A relation needs its own published sentence.
- **Property-based testing (PBT).** PBT-Bench [V~] ([2605.15229](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15229v1)):
  - Agents misuse `assume()` so that it filters out the trigger region, in up to 75% of failures for some models.
  - 31% of failures use the wrong strategy range.
  - The benchmark requires every planted bug to have "a well-defined input region T on which the bug manifests
    with probability one".
  - Hypothesis's `derandomize=True` makes a run deterministic, and `database` controls replay
    ([docs](https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorial/settings.html)) [V~].
  - Agentic PBT over 100 packages found 56% of reports to be valid bugs [S]
    ([2510.09907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09907)).

### 1.4 Selection bias: tuning tests against observed outputs

- **Adaptive reuse invalidates a holdout.**
  - Dwork et al., *The reusable holdout* (Science 2015) [V~]
    ([Google Research summary](https://research.google/blog/the-reusable-holdout-preserving-validity-in-adaptive-data-analysis/)):
    "the moment a team modifies their model based on a previously observed holdout score, they create a
    dependency … that invalidates the assumption of the classic holdout method."
  - Thresholdout keeps estimates valid for a number of adaptive queries that grows roughly with the square of
    the holdout size. It works by limiting what each query reveals.
  - The Ladder (Blum and Hardt, ICML 2015) [S] ([1502.04585](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04585)) releases a new
    score only when it improves significantly. That coarse feedback is what keeps the leaderboard honest.
    `harden.py`'s "aggregate per-check rates only" is a Ladder-like coarsening of the same kind [I].
- **Reuse in practice is often milder than worst case**, when it is the *model* being adapted to a *fixed*
  test set.
  - Roelofs et al. found little overfitting across 100+ Kaggle competitions [S]
    ([NeurIPS 2019](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2019/file/ee39e503b6bedf0c98c388b7e8589aca-Paper.pdf)).
  - Recht et al. found 3-15% (CIFAR-10) and 11-14% (ImageNet) drops on new test sets, "not caused by
    adaptivity" [V~] ([1902.10811](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10811)).
  - Mania et al. attribute the robustness to model similarity [V~]
    ([1905.12580](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12580)).
  - **Our case is the opposite and much stronger [I].** We pick the *test* per item while looking at *each*
    output. With unlimited case mining, the in-sample pass rate can be driven to zero just by finding any
    idiosyncratic flaw in each of the 5 episodes. None of the comforting Kaggle evidence transfers.
- **Adversarial filtering is harder but not fair.** Phang et al. [V~]
  ([2111.08181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08181)) found that adversarially filtered evaluation sets are harder,
  but:
  - rankings become "unstable and highly sensitive to the choice of adversary model";
  - the filtering "oversamples examples with low annotator agreement";
  - it is "disproportionately affecting the adversary model".
  - **Mapped to us [I]:** cases mined from Fable's outputs will bite Fable hardest. They will also concentrate
    on the sentences that are least determinate, which is the same lesson as PatchDiff's 66.2%.
- **Winner's curse.**
  - SIREN [V~] ([2605.05973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05973)) shows that winner-based reporting after tuning
    on benchmark items is optimistic. Its fix is to split selection from held-out evaluation and *freeze the
    shortlist* before measuring.
  - With 5 episodes, P(at most 2/5 observed | true rate 0.6) = **0.317** [M, exact binomial]. A step that looks
    "under the bar" on a screen often is not.
  - The reverse also holds. In math-reasoning work, 10.3-22.9% of items with zero pass@k were actually solvable
    [V~] ([2606.19636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19636)), so 0/k is not evidence of impossibility.
- **Optional stopping.** Monitoring fixed-horizon tests continuously and stopping when they look good makes
  them "wholly unreliable"; always-valid inference is the alternative [V~]
  ([1512.04922](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.04922)). Our confirmation must be fixed-horizon and registered, which
  `research.py` already does.
- **Held-out suites in program repair.** Smith et al. (FSE 2015) [S]
  ([pdf](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~brun/pubs/pubs/Smith15fse.pdf)) established that repair quality must be
  judged on an *independent* test suite. The same logic, applied to episodes rather than tests, is the fresh
  cohort.
- **IRT task selection.** Efficient benchmarking of agents [V~]
  ([2603.23749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23749)) keeps tasks with historical pass rates of 30-70% to preserve
  ranking fidelity, cutting 44-70% of tasks. This is irrelevant to our *goal* (near-zero), but it is a reminder
  that near-zero tasks measure nothing comparative.

## §2 Does a fresh-cohort confirmation make mining from captured outputs legitimate?

`harden.py` (module docstring and step 6 of `HARDENING_BRIEF`) says that only aggregate per-check pass rates
reach the next attempt, "never solver answers or per-episode rewards", and that a revision may not "tune
against solver answers". That rule does three jobs:

1. **Statistical.** It keeps the measured rate from being fitted to the sample, as the Ladder does.
2. **Validity.** It stops a designer from writing tests that encode one solver's idiosyncrasy, or that settle a
   point the instruction leaves open.
3. **Leakage.** A redesign rewrites the instruction and fixture, so solver answers must never flow into what
   the next solver reads.

Fast hardening changes job 3 completely. Nothing the solver sees changes, so there is no channel back to the
solver. Jobs 1 and 2 need separate answers.

**Verdict [I].** A held-out fresh cohort fully repairs job 1 and does nothing for job 2.

- **The number (job 1).**
  - Let T* be the suite mined using cohort M. Its pass rate re-graded on M is a biased, in-sample estimate. It
    is optimistic about hardness and can reach 0 by construction.
  - A cohort C of new episodes, run on the byte-identical agent surface *after* T* is frozen, is independent of
    T*. Its pass rate is unbiased for the true pass rate under T*.
  - This is exactly `measurement.extreme_confirmation(..., held_out=True)` ("confirm only with a predeclared
    fixed cohort of fresh episodes on the frozen final candidate").
  - So mining from captured outputs is **statistically legitimate if and only if** the only numbers that leave
    the lineage as claims come from a fresh cohort. In-sample numbers are labelled provisional.
- **The meaning (job 2).** Confirmation only measures how often fresh episodes fail T*. It cannot tell whether
  failing T* is a capability failure.
  - A test that enforces one reading of an ambiguous sentence fails fresh episodes *reliably*.
  - Mining from outputs *selects for* such tests. The places where a strong solver's output departs from the
    reference are enriched in under-specification: PatchDiff 66.2% uncertain, Phang's low-agreement
    oversampling, the OpenAI audit's 35.5% narrow tests.
  - So mined cases need **stronger** determinacy safeguards than designer-authored ones. Those safeguards must
    not depend on the confirmation (§3 steps 3, 4 and 7).

**Recommended restatement of the principle (not a code change) [I].**

1. **Solver answers never reach anything a solver reads.** The instruction, fixture and environment stay
   frozen; the surface digest refuses any change.
2. **Solver answers reach the hidden suite only through the mined-case protocol** (§3). It emits generic cases
   within published rules. Expected values come from consensus execution, and every case is proved by a mutant
   and adjudicated.
3. **Any rate computed on the cohort a case was mined from is provisional.** Claims require a fresh,
   registered, fixed-horizon cohort.
4. **The redesign path keeps the original rule unchanged.** The designer still sees only aggregate per-check
   rates, including the fast-hardened rates.

**Three tiers of independence, cheapest first [I].**

| tier | how cases are chosen | status of the re-grade of the screen episodes |
|---|---|---|
| **A: blind** | Cases come from rule generators, mutants of the reference and alternative (STING/SWE-ABS style), and `blind_solve` near misses. Episode outputs are **never read**, and the **inclusion rule is fixed before re-grading**: keep every case that passes consensus, validator and mutant proof | **Out-of-sample**: the tests are independent of the episodes. It is still n = 5 per model, so it is reported with exact bounds. Choosing *which* blind cases to keep by their episode kills turns it into tier B |
| **B-cross: cross-family** | Mined from model X's captured episodes only | Out-of-sample **for model Y's** episodes, and in-sample for X. This mirrors EvalPlus's leave-one-out over LLMs |
| **B: episode-informed** | Differential runs of captured episodes against the consensus (PatchDiff/UTBoost style) | **In-sample, provisional**. Needs a fresh cohort |

Report every tier separately. Never pool a tier-B number with a tier-A or confirmed one.

## §3 Design: hidden-only fast hardening (`fastharden`)

Two pieces already exist. `sandbox.grade_capture` re-grades a captured tree with a bundle's *current* verifier,
and its docstring already names `fastharden`. `genuine.py` certifies every failed check and re-grades to show
it reproduces. What follows is a protocol over existing parts. The pipeline pieces are named in brackets.

### Step 0. Freeze and fingerprint the agent surface (under 1 min)

- Compute `agent_surface_sha256` over everything the agent sees:
  - `environment/` (Dockerfile, toolchain, deps manifests, repo);
  - every `steps/*/instruction.md`;
  - the agent-side `task.toml` fields (timeouts, resources, network);
  - the harness identity (`artifacts.harness_identity`);
  - the model ids and effort of each screened campaign.

  `oracle.public_inventory` already hashes `environment/` and the instructions.
- **Refuse** if any byte differs from the screened version. Route the task to the full redesign instead.
- Also assert two things:
  - the harness cannot skip steps on reward (`benchmark/harbor.py` already refuses `min_reward`);
  - the verifier is `separate`, so no grade reaches the agent between steps.

  Both make "behaviour does not depend on hidden tests" true by construction.
- **Re-grade fidelity check.** Re-grade 2-3 captured steps with the *old* verifier image, and require the
  stored `arena-grade.json` verdicts to come back identically. This proves that a re-grade equals a live grade
  for this task before any new number is trusted.

### Step 1. Targets (seconds)

- From the screen's per-check rates, take the steps above `policy.STEP_PASS_RATE_MAX` on either model and, in
  each, the *fully passing* episodes.
- Only those episodes can move a step's pass@1. Adding cases to a check that already fails changes nothing.
- List the published sentences (`rule_quote`s) of the step's checks. These are the *only* rules a mined case
  may enforce, because fast hardening cannot publish anything new.

### Step 2. Candidate mining within the published rules (5-15 min, mostly LLM)

The miner writes **generators** and **properties**, not literal cases (LiveCodeBench, CodeContests+). Sources,
in order of independence:

1. **Rule generators** (tier A) [`author/cases.py`]:
   - `intersections` over the dimensions each quoted sentence names (boundary x boundary, rare intersections,
     alias or format variants for the Astra-type M3 failure, scope siblings for the Fable-type M1 failure);
   - `sequences` for state or rebuild rules.
2. **Mutant differentials** (tier A).
   - New LLM semantic mutants of the reference and the alternative: condition changes first, since they are
     52-54% of STING survivors.
   - Existing `m_baseline_`/`m_partial_` mutants.
   - `blind_solve` diffs.
   - Search generated inputs for those that separate each mutant from the consensus.
3. **Metamorphic relations** (tier A), only where a sentence publishes the relation: order independence,
   idempotent re-run, alias equivalence, round trip. The relation is the oracle, so no expected value is
   needed.
4. **Episode differentials** (tier B). Run each target episode's captured tree and the consensus on the
   generated inputs, and keep the inputs where they differ (PatchDiff, intramorphic).
   - The miner may read the episode's code to localise the divergence.
   - It may **emit only a generic case over the published interface**: no file names, identifiers, helper
     names or message strings that only that episode introduced (see the step 6 scan).

Every generated input must pass an **input validator written from the instruction and visible fixture alone**
(CodeContests+, auditors, EvalPlus contracts). The validator records why the input is in the published domain:
types, ranges, the formats the fixture shows or the instruction names. A case outside the domain is dropped,
not argued.

### Step 3. Consensus oracle: the expected value is executed, never typed (2-10 min)

For each candidate input:

1. **Run the implementations.** Run the **reference** (`solution/`), the **alternative**
   (`authoring/alt/<step>.patch`, ideally from `independent_oracle` with its public-input provenance) and, for
   every tier-B case, a **third implementation from a different vendor**. The third is a `blind_solve` output
   that passes every pre-existing check. Each runs **three times** in the verifier image.
2. **Keep a case only if all agree.** Agreement is byte-equality of the canonicalised output through the
   published interface. Where a sentence publishes an equivalence ("any order", "case-insensitive"), the
   agreement predicate *is* that equivalence, and the test becomes a **checker**, not an equality.
3. **Disagreement is never settled by "the reference wins".** It means one of two things:
   - a spec gap: drop the case;
   - a reference or alternative bug: escalate as a validity finding, because the controls passed a defective
     reference.
4. **The expected value is a derived artifact.** It is written by a deterministic generator script to
   `tests/suites/data/mined/<step>/…` with its provenance (generator seed, input sha, reference, alternative and
   third patch shas, verifier image digest, command), and recorded in `authoring/mining/consensus.json`.
   - `assurance.generated_problems` regenerates it during validation, and any differing byte blocks. That is how
     "derived, never stored by hand" stays checkable.
   - Prefer **relational assertions** (property or metamorphic) wherever the rule is relational. They need no
     expected value at all.
5. **Flake filter for differentiating cases** (PatchDiff). The consensus must pass the case in every run, and a
   failing implementation must fail it reproducibly. Use 3 runs per side by default; raise it for any case that
   touches time, ordering, concurrency or the filesystem.

### Step 4. Determinacy probe: guarding against common-mode misreading (2-5 min, parallel)

Agreement among the reference, the alternative and the third implementation cannot catch a shared misreading
(N-version: cross-vendor pairs perfectly correlated in 87 of 158). So for every kept case, two models from
**two vendors**, neither of them the miner, get **only** three things:

- the instruction(s) up to that step;
- the visible fixture;
- the case input.

**They do not get the expected output.** Each must:

1. predict the output, or choose between the consensus output and the divergent output with the labels hidden;
2. quote the sentence that decides it;
3. state whether any *other* output is also consistent with the instruction.

Keep the case only if both predict the consensus and name a sentence the check's `rule_quote` contains, and
neither finds a second consistent output. This is the automated counterpart of PatchDiff's manual
"uncertain" class. It is advisory to the reviewer but blocking for *mined* cases [I].

### Step 5. Mutant proof and robustness (3-8 min, incremental controls)

- **Mutant per trap.** Every mined case lands in a check (re-aiming a free check, or a new one within the band
  limits). Each check has an executed mutant that fails exactly its declared checks, passes at least one
  objective and never scores 1.0. That is the existing control.
- **Tier-B independence.** A tier-B case must also kill **a mutant that was not derived from the episode that
  suggested it** (an `m_baseline_` idiom, or another family's near miss). A case that kills only the one
  episode it came from is presumed idiosyncratic (§4 R5).
- **Robustness** (STING). The reference, the alternative and LLM-made behaviour-preserving rewrites of the
  reference (renaming, restructuring, reordering independent statements) must all pass. This kills tests keyed
  to implementation details (SWE-ABS 10.6%, OpenAI 35.5%).
- **Monotone only.** No check is deleted, weakened or tolerance-widened. No `requires` is added. Weights change
  only within band room (existing `harden.py` rules).
- **Full validation.** The bundle validation runs as usual: controls (nop, golden x3, alternative, mutants,
  empty, destructive, test_tamper, early_exit, report_forge), `hidden.disjoint`, leak sweep and
  `assurance.generated_problems`. Fast hardening skips none of it, because it is the guarantee that the
  shipped grader still works.

### Step 6. Re-grade the captured episodes (2-6 min)

1. **Grade every captured step.** For every valid screen trial, grade every step in order with
   `sandbox.grade_capture(new_bundle, step, steps/<s>/artifacts/app, history={earlier captures})`. Use the new
   verifier image and run up to three times; re-run only on a changed verdict.
2. **Monotonicity check.** For every episode, the set of failing checks under the new suite must contain the
   old set. An old-fail → new-pass means a weakened check or nondeterminism, and it **blocks**.
3. **Certify every newly failed check** with `genuine.py` (assertion, repo_crash or output; reproduces).
   Anything `suspect` or `interface` stays uncounted until a human adjudicates it.
4. **Episode-literal scan [I].** Collect tokens and string literals that appear in a tier-B source episode's
   diff but in none of the instruction, the fixture, the reference or the alternative. Any such token in the new
   tests is a finding: the test is keyed to that episode.
5. **Write `verification/fastharden/<round>/regrade.json`** with:
   - per episode and step: old and new reward and newly failed checks;
   - per case: the episodes and mutants it kills and the model family of each kill;
   - each case's tier;
   - per step and model: the full-pass rate, labelled `provisional_in_sample`, `cross_held_out` or `blind`.

### Step 7. Near-miss adjudication (2-5 min)

- **Every newly failing episode is read against its failing case**: the episode's output, the consensus output
  and the deciding sentence (`policy.ALL_FAIL_PROOF_OBLIGATIONS`: "no near miss is unadjudicated").
- The adjudicator is an LLM of a different vendor from the episode's model, with escalation to a human on
  disagreement. It answers: "Is the episode's behaviour a defensible reading of the published sentence?"
- **If yes, the case is removed and the round re-run.** One defensible reading is enough to void a case, even if
  four other episodes fail it legitimately. Phang's lesson: mined sets concentrate on contested items.

### Step 8. What the review must check (semantic review, 5-15 min)

The review is "the last honest reader". For a fast-hardened revision it must confirm all of the following:

1. **Surface frozen.** `agent_surface_sha256` matches the screened version, and the old-verifier re-grade
   fidelity check passed.
2. **Every new case is published.**
   - Its `rule_quote` is verbatim, and that sentence alone settles the case; the reviewer re-reads it without
     the consensus output.
   - The input passes the instruction-only validator and is absent from the repository.
   - It is no "may" enforced as "must", and no wider than the instruction's scope (the OpenAI "wide test"
     class).
3. **Oracle provenance.**
   - The expected values regenerate byte-identically from the recorded generator and patch shas.
   - The reference, the alternative and the third implementation agree.
   - No literal in the tests equals a consensus output unless the generator wrote it.
4. **Interface and independence.**
   - Tests observe only through the published interface.
   - No name is introduced only by the reference or by an episode.
   - The episode-literal scan is clean, the robustness variants pass, and each tier-B case kills an
     episode-independent mutant.
5. **Determinacy and adjudication records** are complete for every case and every newly failing episode, and
   every voided case is removed.
6. **Concentration (advisory).** Kills per case by model family, and the share of the step's new failures that
   comes from cases mined from that same family (Phang). Model-vs-model comparisons are never made on a mined
   suite.
7. **Accounting.** The round number on this mining cohort, and the label on every reported rate.

### Step 9. Fresh-cohort confirmation, and how to report it

**Registration (before running).**

- Use the existing `arena research prepare` → `validate` → `run` → `report`.
- The plan freezes:
  - the agent-surface digest and the **new verifier digest**;
  - the model ids, harness and effort;
  - **n per model, fixed in advance**;
  - the analysis: per-step full-pass rates and the whole-task pass@1, with exact one-sided Clopper-Pearson upper
    bounds, Bonferroni across the 2 models (`measurement.success_observation` at α = 0.05/2), and also across
    steps if "every step" is claimed at once.
- Scored episodes cannot be dropped. Voids are classified, never silently excluded.

**Free confirmation [I].** Because the agent surface is unchanged, the **calibration of record**
(`arena qualify`, `policy.SPEC_EPISODES_PER_MODEL` = 10 per model) can *be* the confirmation cohort, as long as
the suite was frozen and the plan registered before it ran. Confirmation then costs nothing beyond what
shipping already costs.

**Once used, a cohort is spent for confirmation.**

- Its episodes may become the next round's mining cohort.
- Never re-mine on a confirmation cohort and then re-confirm on it.
- If confirmation comes out worse than the provisional number, report both. Do not mine again and re-confirm
  on the same cohort (no optional stopping).

**What each cohort size can claim.** Exact one-sided upper bounds at α = 0.025 per model (joint 95% over two
models) [M, computed]:

| fresh n per model | 0 passes | 1 pass | 2 passes | 3 passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.522 | 0.716 | 0.853 | 0.947 |
| 10 | 0.308 | 0.445 | 0.556 | 0.652 |
| 15 | 0.218 | 0.319 | 0.405 | 0.481 |
| 20 | 0.168 | 0.249 | 0.317 | 0.379 |
| 30 | 0.116 | 0.172 | 0.221 | 0.265 |
| 72 | 0.050 | 0.075 | 0.097 | 0.117 |

- **Below the 0.5 per-step bar.** Confirming ≤ 0.5 needs n ≥ 6 at 0 passes, 9 at 1 pass, 12 at 2 passes and
  15 at 3 passes.
- **The extreme target 0.05** needs **n ≥ 72 per model at 0 passes**, or 110 at 1 pass. At affordable sizes
  that target is only ever *observed*, never confirmed, and the report must say so.
  `harden.extreme_target_met`'s docstring already does ("observed stopping target, not a statistically
  confirmed hardness claim").

**Report table** (one row per step and model):

| step | model | screen (old suite) | provisional in-sample (tier B) | cross-held-out (tier B-cross) | blind re-grade (tier A) | confirmed: fresh n, passes, joint 95% upper |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules for the table:

- Only the last column may support a difficulty claim, and only at the strength its bound supports.
- Every zero carries its `genuine.py` classification.
- Alongside the table, report:
  - the mining yield (candidates generated, dropped by validator, consensus, determinacy, flake, robustness and
    adjudication, then kept);
  - the number of fast rounds spent on this cohort;
  - the per-case kill concentration.

**A null yield is a result [I].** If the step's free episodes satisfy every determinate input the generators
reach, fast hardening finds nothing. That is evidence the step's published rules are realised correctly, and
the step needs a *redesign* (a new rule), not more tests. The literature's yields set expectations:

- UTBoost strengthened only 5-8% of instances;
- STING added tests on 42% (211/500);
- SWE-ABS strengthened 50%.

A task we designed adversarially will be at the low end of that range for its already-measured steps.

**Budget accounting [I].**

- Fast rounds do not change the agent surface, so they need not spend the redesign budget
  (`MAX_HARDENING_ATTEMPTS`).
- They are adaptive on the mining cohort, so record them in `hardened/hardening.json` as a separate,
  never-reset counter.
- Cap them per mining cohort: two rounds is a reasonable default. After that, the provisional number on that
  cohort says little, and the next information has to come from a fresh cohort.

## §4 Main validity risks and safeguards

| # | risk | evidence | safeguard (§3 step) |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **In-sample optimism**: tests tuned to the 5 observed episodes; the re-graded rate falls by construction | Reusable holdout and Ladder; SIREN; UTBoost and PatchDiff re-grades are in-sample | Tiers A, B-cross and B labelled separately; fresh registered fixed-horizon cohort for any claim; cap on rounds per cohort (step 9) |
| R2 | **Ambiguity enrichment**: mined divergences concentrate on under-specified sentences | PatchDiff 66.2% uncertain; Phang low-agreement oversampling; OpenAI 59.4% flawed on hard tasks | Instruction-only validator (2); two-vendor determinacy probe with the expected output withheld (4); near-miss adjudication where one defensible reading voids the case (7); review item 2 |
| R3 | **Common-mode oracle error**: reference and alternative share a misreading | Knight-Leveson; N-version agents (87 of 158 cross-agent pairs with ϕ = 1; the circumcircle misreading); HumanEval >10% wrong ground truths | Third implementation from another vendor for tier B; determinacy probe; relational assertions where possible; disagreement is never settled for the reference (3, 4) |
| R4 | **Narrow tests**: implementation details the instruction never required | OpenAI 35.5%; SWE-ABS 53/500 gold-specific; STING robustness dropped 23% of candidates | Published interface only; behaviour-preserving rewrites of the reference must pass; alternative passes; no reference-only names (5, review 4) |
| R5 | **Episode-keyed tests**: a case encodes one episode's quirk (a file name, a message, a helper) | (inferred from R4) | Episode-literal scan (6); each tier-B case must kill an episode-independent mutant (5); a case killing a single episode and nothing else is presumed idiosyncratic |
| R6 | **Wide tests**: enforcing behaviour beyond the instruction's scope | OpenAI "wide" tests 18.8% [S]; "may enforced as must" anti-lever (CODING-DIFFICULTY-LEVERS §1.3) | `rule_quote` required and re-read without the output; only published sentences may be enforced (1, review 2) |
| R7 | **Re-grade ≠ live grade**: capture gaps, `/opt/deps` relink, time or locale dependence, flaky verdicts | PatchDiff's 20-run filter; Harbor's separate verifier sees only the captures | Old-verifier fidelity re-grade (0); 3x re-grades; monotonicity old ⊆ new failing set (6); flake filter on differentiating cases (3) |
| R8 | **Agent-surface drift**: any change to fixture, instruction, environment, toolchain, deps or harness | — | `agent_surface_sha256` refusal (0); any change routes to redesign plus a full screen |
| R9 | **Model drift between the mining and confirmation cohorts** (provider updates) | — | The confirmation plan freezes model ids and harness identity; a changed identity is reported as a different cohort, never pooled |
| R10 | **Per-family bias**: cases mined from Astra's outputs bite Astra | Phang: "disproportionately affecting the adversary model" | Report kills by family and the mining source of each case (review 6); never compare the models on a mined suite; mine from both families' episodes |
| R11 | **Void counted as failure**: grader, infra or interface errors | CLAUDE.md "every zero is classified"; TB2 grader-bug zeros (W1) | `genuine.py` certificate for each newly failed check; `suspect`/`interface` uncounted until adjudicated (6) |
| R12 | **New attack surface from new hidden data or tests** | ANTI_REWARD_HACKING §7 | `hidden.disjoint`, `test_tamper`, `early_exit` and report-forge controls re-run in full (5); the trajectory scan is unchanged because the episodes are unchanged |
| R13 | **Shared mistake counted as independent difficulty** | CLAUDE.md "several checks can reveal one shared mistake" | Report at the step full-pass level; per-case kills are diagnostics, not independent probabilities |
| R14 | **"Impossible" read into 0/k** | Sampling blind spot: 10-23% of 0/k items solvable | `ALL_FAIL_PROOF_OBLIGATIONS` (alternative passes, executed mutant, reached, near misses adjudicated); bounds instead of "0%" |
| R15 | **Optional stopping on confirmation** | Always-valid inference (Johari et al.) | Registered n; the `research.py` fixed horizon; no re-mining on a confirmation cohort |
| R16 | **Selecting which steps to fast-harden off a 5-episode screen** (winner's curse in reverse) | P(at most 2/5 \| p = 0.6) = 0.317 | Treat "under the bar on the screen" as provisional too; the confirmation covers every step, not only the hardened ones |

## §5 Expected time budget per step

All figures are wall-clock for one step of one task with 5 x 2 captured episodes, parallel where noted. Where
an anchor was measured, it is marked [M]; the other figures are [I] estimates from the anchors in §1.5.

| phase | estimate | anchor |
|---|---|---|
| 0 surface digest, old-verifier fidelity re-grade (2-3 captures in parallel) | 1-2 min | verifier median 31.6 s, p90 92.9 s [M] |
| 1 targets from per-check rates | < 1 min | — |
| 2 mining: generators, mutant differentials, episode differentials (LLM agent in the sandbox) | 5-15 min | auditors' median 5.8-22.8 min per problem [V~]; SWE-ABS $4.98 per instance [V~] |
| 3 consensus: 3 implementations x 3 runs over the candidates, batched in one verifier container | 2-10 min | per-grade cost [M]; scales with the number of candidates |
| 4 determinacy probe (2 vendors, short read-only prompts, parallel) | 2-5 min | — |
| 5 mutant proof, robustness variants, then full validation and controls | 3-8 min incremental; 3-20 min if the full suite reruns | full control suite 169-1,168 s, median about 350 s [M] |
| 6 re-grade 10 episodes x steps up to the target step, 3x on changed verdicts, parallel | 2-6 min | 31.6 s median per step-grade [M] |
| 7 near-miss adjudication | 2-5 min | — |
| 8 semantic review | 5-15 min | — |
| **one round, end to end** | **about 25-60 min**, of which the inner "edit tests → rebuild verifier → re-grade 10 captures" loop is **about 3-6 min** | compared with hours for a redesign plus 20-40 min for a re-screen |
| 9 confirmation | 20-40 min (one screen's width at n = 5, longer at n = 10), **or free if it rides on the calibration of record** | brief; `SPEC_EPISODES_PER_MODEL` = 10 |

Under these estimates, the "minutes" are the inner loop and a provisional number. A *claim* still costs one
fresh cohort, but that cohort can be the 10-episode calibration of record the task needs anyway.

## §6 What is not established

- No step of this protocol has been run on one of our tasks. The yields, drop sizes and timings in §1 come from
  SWE-bench and competitive programming on older models, with tests that were not adversarially designed.
- The determinacy probe and the episode-literal scan are design proposals. Their false-positive and
  false-negative rates are unmeasured.
- How much of a tier-B provisional drop survives a fresh cohort is the key unknown. The literature offers no
  measurement of it for agentic coding. The first fast-hardening rounds should therefore always be confirmed,
  so that this "shrinkage" is estimated before anyone relies on provisional numbers.
- The local timing anchors come from step verifier spans and control suites in this workspace's artefacts, not
  from a fast-hardening run.
